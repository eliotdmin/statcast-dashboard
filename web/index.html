How the shrinking works

Three steps, no judgement calls anywhere in them.

01

Split the season and correlate the halves

Odd half-month blocks against even ones, same player. Whatever is real about him shows up in both halves; whatever was luck does not. The correlation between them is the share of the spread that is real.

r = Var(T) / Var(X)
02

Project it to your sample size

That correlation was measured at one particular length. Spearman–Brown converts it to any other: reliability rises with sample, but with diminishing returns, which is why watching longer eventually stops helping.

ρ(k) = kρ₀ / (1 + (k−1)ρ₀)
03

Shrink the percentile by exactly that much

Pull the observed figure toward the league mean, keeping the fraction that is real and discarding the rest. Nothing is tuned here — the shrinkage coefficient and the reliability are the same number.

T̂ = μ + ρ(X − μ)

What this is not

It is not a projection system. Nothing here forecasts next season, and where it was tested against one it did not beat the public systems. It answers a narrower question: of what already happened, how much was real.

Reliability is measured, not assumed — but it is measured on the assumption that true talent held still inside the window, which makes it mildly optimistic over long stretches. Carry rates were fit on 2023–2025 and evaluated once on 2026; 2026 is no longer a clean holdout and is labelled as such wherever it appears.

Data. Statcast pitch-level, 2023–2026, refreshed daily at 11:00 by a launchd job that fetches only days it has never seen and runs an integrity check every time.
Method. Reliability is a split-half correlation corrected by Spearman–Brown; percentiles are shrunk toward the league at exactly that rate.
