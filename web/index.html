Luck-adjusted percentiles

How did a player’s output compare with his peers’, and is it real?

Pick any player and any date range, and see where he ranked against everyone with similar playing time. Every number is adjusted for how much of it is luck at that sample size, so a hot six weeks doesn’t pass for a breakout. Matching players with the most similar stretch come along with it.

Swing & approach changes

How did a player’s underlying skills change, and are the changes real or fluky?

Flags every time a player’s bat speed, attack angle, chase rate or other inputs moved further than chance allows against his own earlier season — often weeks before it shows up in results. Then it checks, after the fact, whether the change held.

For every qualified player: how far above or below his usual level he’s been over the last month, and how much of that the model expects to last into next month. Two streaks of the same size can have very different outlooks.

Head-to-head odds

Head-to-head matchups between two players.

Pick two players and a window, and get the odds that one finishes ahead of the other — not a projected stat line, because predicting anyone’s exact numbers a month out barely beats guessing. It shows how much of the answer is genuine difference and how much is luck over the plate appearances ahead, and it publishes its own scoring record so you can see what “62%” has actually meant.

The two-week breakout

“He’s earned it” mostly doesn’t last.

Every week someone is smacking the ball — buy him, he’s earned his results. We checked every two-week hot streak from 2023–2025: 928 of them, averaging 129 points of wOBA above the hitter’s own earlier-season rate.

14%of the gain survived the next two weeks
43%were back at or below the hitter’s own baseline right away
13% vs 13%kept by streaks backed by better contact vs. by pure luck — no difference

Over two weeks, even contact quality is mostly noise. What makes a streak believable is how long it lasts: a 30-day hot streak kept 27% of its gain the following month, and a six-week one kept 43% — with or without the hard contact. That is the problem this dashboard is built for: Streak carry forecast says how much of a streak to keep, and Luck-adjusted percentiles shows which numbers are real at the sample you’re looking at.

Why trust it

Built to be honest about uncertainty.

Adjusted for sample size

Every percentile is pulled toward league average by exactly how much of it is noise at your sample size. Nothing is tuned by hand.

Checked after the fact

Flagged changes are scored against what happened next, and the hit rate is shown right next to them.

Clear about limits

Next month’s results are mostly luck. The site says how much of it any model could predict, and doesn’t pretend to more.

What this is not

It is not a season projection system. Where its methods were tested against one, they did not beat the public systems. It answers narrower questions: of what already happened, how much was real, and how much of it is likely to last.

Carry rates were fit on 2023–2025 and scored on 2026, which has since been looked at too many times to count as a clean test — read those figures as optimistic. Details and the predictions frozen against 2027 are on the Method page.
